Reading damage from space, one building at a time

A model compares each building's satellite image from before and after the earthquakes and estimates how badly it was damaged. It was trained on five cities and is tested here on Antakya, a city it never saw during training or tuning.

Before and after

Each building is cut out of a pre-event image (BRIGHT, Maxar) and the earliest usable post-event Maxar image, shifted onto the same grid and co-registered. Drag the line to compare. The outline is the building footprint the model is asked about.

How well does it work?

Macro-F1 averages the F1 score of the three classes, so the rare damaged and destroyed buildings count as much as the many intact ones. "Top 10 %": share of destroyed buildings found by inspecting the 10 % of buildings the method ranks highest. ECE: calibration error of the probabilities after temperature scaling (lower is better).

Where it goes wrong

What this cannot do

  • Satellites see roofs. A building whose lower floors collapsed can look intact from above. An "intact" prediction is never a safety assessment.
  • "Damaged" is the hardest class. Its visual signature is subtle and its boundary with the other classes is fuzzy even for experts.
  • Labels are expert gradings from imagery, not ground inspections, and the earliest gradings under-report damage in places.
  • One event, one region. Building stock, imagery and labelling differ elsewhere.
