Istanbul · preparing, not predicting

Istanbul, before the next one

The model was tested on a city it had never seen. Istanbul has no post-earthquake images and no damage labels, so its accuracy there cannot be measured. What can be done now is to have everything else ready: every mapped building, the official scenario estimates per neighbourhood, and a pipeline that turns the first post-event images into a list of buildings to check first.

964 neighbourhoods

Each shape is a mahalle. Scenario shows İBB's estimate of very heavily and heavily damaged buildings for a Mw 7.5 night-time earthquake. Old stock is the share of buildings built before 1980 (İBB, 2017). Footprints compares the buildings mapped in OpenStreetMap with İBB's count: the image model can only score buildings that have a footprint.

On the day

  1. Before images arrive. The scenario layer and the building inventory say where heavy damage is most likely and how many buildings each mahalle has.
  2. First post-event scene. Released scenes (for example through the Maxar Open Data Program, as in 2023) are cut into 600 m cells on the local UTM grid at 0.35 m and aligned to a pre-event reference image.
  3. Every mapped building is scored with the model trained on the 2023 earthquakes. Probabilities are calibrated with the settings fitted on the validation city.
  4. Output: a ranking, not a verdict. Buildings sorted by expected severity, a GeoJSON of the first few thousand for a field map, and a per-mahalle summary next to the scenario numbers. In Antakya, the top 10 % of such a ranking held about half of all destroyed buildings.
python -m enkaz.respond --inventory data/istanbul/buildings.npz \
    --post scene.tif --pre reference.tif \
    --checkpoint post_only/best.pt --calibration post_only/eval.json \
    --out response/

What this is not

  • Not a forecast of the model's accuracy in Istanbul. Building stock, roof types and image conditions differ from the 2023 cities; a few hundred quickly graded buildings would be needed to check thresholds on the day.
  • The scenario is a planning estimate from İBB's loss model, not an observation, and it is shown here for one earthquake scenario only.
  • Footprints are incomplete. Where OpenStreetMap has far fewer buildings than İBB counts, the missing ones are invisible to the pipeline. Open machine-made footprints could fill the gaps.
  • Names, not codes. İBB's tables are joined to OpenStreetMap boundaries by district and mahalle name; 937 of 959 rows match, the rest are left out rather than guessed.
