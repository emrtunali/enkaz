Istanbul · a scenario, not a forecast

If the Marmara fault breaks

A magnitude 7.5 earthquake on the 104 km of the North Anatolian Fault that runs under the Sea of Marmara, 15–25 km off Istanbul's southern coast, at night. What follows is what published models of ground shaking, buildings and people say would happen, run 200 times to show how sure they are.

Streets, hospitals, bridges, rail

A collapsing building throws debris about half its height into the street. Where less than 3 metres stay open, an ambulance or fire engine cannot pass. In Istanbul's narrow residential streets that happens often; on main roads, which are wide, rarely.

How this is built, and what it leaves out

  • Rupture. The Main Marmara Fault from the Central Marmara Basin to Çınarcık, as mapped in GEM's Global Active Faults database (EMME catalogue): 104 km, vertical, right-lateral, 0–20 km deep, Mw 7.5, the magnitude İBB uses.
  • Ground motion. Akkar, Sandıkkaya & Bommer (2014) and Boore et al. (2014), equal weight, computed with OpenQuake. 100 earthquakes per model, each with its own between-event term and a spatially correlated within-event field (Jayaram & Baker 2009), correlated across periods (Baker & Jayaram 2008). Site conditions from topographic slope (Wald & Allen 2007) on the Copernicus DEM.
  • Buildings. 1.2 million footprints (OpenStreetMap + Microsoft). Building types from the European Seismic Risk Model ESRM20 for Istanbul, reweighted per mahalle by İBB's data on building age and height; storeys from OpenStreetMap where tagged. Damage and deaths from ESRM20's fragility and loss-of-life functions; 15.1 million night-time residents spread over floor area.
  • Streets and hospitals. 20,700 km of OSM roads; 417 hospitals; debris reach 0.5 × building height, 3 m minimum passage; travel at typical speeds by road type, without traffic.
  • Not modelled: tsunami, landslides, liquefaction, fires, aftershocks, damage to bridges, tunnels, pipelines and power, traffic and evacuation, and day-time occupancy.
